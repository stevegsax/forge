## Problem

With the batch transport replaced, the decision record no longer matches
the system: D77/D78 record the superseded signal-wait design, D80's
audit-only claim about `batch_jobs` only now becomes true, D81 references
the deleted poller Schedule (and carries a 600s-vs-60s doc drift), and
pbook's TEMPORAL_PATTERNS rule 8 lacks the scoping that makes timer-loop
polling safe at scale.

## Background and Detailed Explanation

In this project the written decision record is authoritative: DECISIONS.md
is where the system's design is described as the status-of-record, and it
is append-only, so nothing in it is ever edited away — corrections land as
dated *banners* stacked on top of the original text, keeping the history
truthful. That discipline has a cost. When T4.1 and T4.2 rewrote the batch
transport, they left the record describing a system that no longer existed,
and a stale record is a real liability when it is the thing people trust.
This task was the reconciliation pass — no code, just one careful sweep
over the decision record and pbook's Temporal-patterns guide to make the
writing match the shipped machinery.

The specific drifts were catalogued up front: D77 and D78 recorded the
superseded signal-wait design; D80's claim that `batch_jobs` is an
audit-only ledger only *became* true once the poller stopped writing live
status to it; D81 pointed at a poller Schedule that no longer exists (and
carried a stale 600s-vs-60s default); and pbook's TEMPORAL_PATTERNS rule 8
lacked the scoping that keeps timer-loop polling safe at scale — an
interval floor, the per-iteration history cost, and continue-as-new as the
escape hatch if wait counts ever grow. A mid-task amendment widened the
scope to fold in decisions that T4.1/T4.2 had made but recorded only in
their own Dev Notes; they pre-dated the PROCESS.md rule (added the same
day) that now requires a decision banner to land in the same change-set as
the change. Those folded-in calls became the substance of a new entry,
**D100**: the shared `sax_platform.temporal.polling` primitive, ocr's
`BackoffSchedule` cadence (which supersedes D88's "default 300–600s"
language), forge's anthropic-only transport, the `MistralOcr.poll_batch`
split, and the derived timeout tree — a decision-level mechanism that until
then had no decision entry at all.

The task's most useful finding came from a deliberate verify-before-touch
pass: much of the scoped work was already done. D77, D78, and D81 already
carried their supersession banners, stamped back when D88 was first
written, so the real remaining work was narrower than the scope implied —
reaffirmation banners on D76 and D79, an as-shipped note under D80, the
`Amended by D100` banner on D88, the D100 entry itself, and pbook's rule-8
amendment. One inconsistency was left in place on purpose: D80's original
Decision body still reads "all coordination uses Temporal signals" in the
present tense. Because the record is append-only, that sentence is
preserved as written and corrected by the two banners now sitting above it,
rather than being edited to read as though it had always been right.

### Amendment (2026-07-20): fold in the T4.1/T4.2 implementation-time decisions

The T4.1/T4.2 implementations made decision-level calls recorded only in
their task files' Dev Notes (pre-dating the PROCESS.md "Specification
Changes" policy, added 2026-07-20, which now requires banners at change
time). This sweep folds them into the decision record:

1. **D80 restoration must match what shipped:** the `batch_jobs` reduction
   was behavioral, not structural — write points are submit + terminal
   outcome (`PersistBatchOutcome`, written by the waiter; cross-queue by
   ocr), `update_batch_status` is monotonic (WHERE-guard, error-preserve),
   statuses are submitted → ended | failed | expired | missing with
   `processing` legacy, and `workflow_id` survives as audit provenance.
   The "spend" half of "audit/spend ledger" is still aspirational — no
   token/model columns exist; record spend columns as considered and
   deferred (candidate future D80 amendment), not implied-shipped.
2. **The D77/D78 supersession entry should record the shared loop:** the
   timer loop is a platform primitive (`sax_platform.temporal.polling`:
   `wait_batch_ended`, `FixedInterval`/`BackoffSchedule`,
   `BATCH_WAIT_CEILING`), consumed by forge and ocr — with the T4.1 replay
   histories as the extraction's determinism proof.
3. **D88's cadence language needs an amendment banner:** it says "default
   300–600s"; ocr's shipped cadence is `BackoffSchedule` — 300s for the
   first hour, doubling to an **1800s cap**, 10% jitter (grounded in the
   T4.2 burst data: one batch per chunk; Mistral publishes no tier
   numbers). The ≥300s floor is honored; the 600s ceiling is not the
   shipped shape. Also record: forge's transport is **anthropic-only**
   (T4.2 ST3; non-anthropic providers rejected), and
   `MistralOcr.poll_batch` was split into `get_batch_status` /
   `fetch_batch_results` (owner-directed, 2026-07-19), eliminating the
   duplicate result download per completed batch.
4. **The timeout tree is a new decision-level mechanism with no D-entry:**
   child + workflow execution timeouts derived from the permitted-wait
   budget (mode-aware `_child_timeout`, `derive_execution_timeout`,
   `MAX_PLAN_STEPS = 25` enforced on `Plan.steps`) — record it in the
   D77/D78 supersession entry or as its own entry; the formulas live in
   the [T4.1 Dev Notes](T4.1-forge-timer-loop-transport.md).
